# Cluster C report: silent numerical faults, converters, compilers, NPUs

Downloads are under .../scratchpad/papers/<id>/. No edits made. Status key: full = read in full from PDF; partial = targeted passes; abstract = proceedings page or abstract only (cite=no).

## Short answers

1. **Has anyone shown a vendor toolchain silently breaking a model that compiles and runs, at our scale (0% accuracy, constant output)?** Yes, as a phenomenon, and one paper hits our scale on one model.
   - Shin (2609.16085): an externally quantized ResNet-50 sent to Qualcomm AI Hub compiles, profiles and runs on device without error. Top-1 falls from 0.75 to 0.005 because the HTP ignores the external QDQ scales. It also reports a zero-copy output-buffer aliasing bug that took top-1 to 0.0014 "with no error". Measured, one model, one phone class.
   - DeltaNN and Louloudakis report up to 100% label disagreement after converters that did not crash (ResNet101, TFLite to Keras). Measured, one-pass CNNs, label agreement with the source model, not accuracy.
   - Jajal et al. find that 33% of ONNX-converter failure issues are "Wrong Model" (semantically incorrect output).
   - Nobody has done this at our breadth (35 phones, 15 faulty configurations, a positional NPU-only fault, a TFLite Micro REDUCE_SUM constant output, TensorRT explicit-QDQ 0% on four models) or on looped models. We cannot claim to be first to show the phenomenon.
2. **Has anyone tied fault size to iteration?** Not for toolchain faults. For quantization of recurrent models, yes: ETH (2607.16237) and LoopQ say error accumulates over recursion steps. Our own ICLR submission (2609.39277) says accumulation holds only for loops that never settle. That contradicts our "errors compound across loops" explanation (see Risk 1).
3. **Has anyone detected broken builds from an early internal state against a host reference, label-free?** Not as a build-level detector with a reported AUROC in anything I found. Layer-by-layer comparison against a reference exists (Louloudakis, NNSmith bisection, CRADLE). It is used to localize a fault after labels or outputs already disagree. This is a negative from about ten searches, not an exhaustive one.
4. **Strongest honest novelty claim:** see the last section.

## Per-paper entries

### 1. Schlögl, Hofer, Böhme, "Causes and Effects of Unanticipated Numerical Deviations in Neural Network Inference Frameworks", NeurIPS 2023
- Venue: NeurIPS 2023 main, verified on papers.neurips.cc. Read: full main text; supplement not read. Code: uibk-iNNference on GitHub. Affiliation: Univ. Innsbruck; the group had earlier refereed forensics and iNNformant work. No slop flags. **Grade: anchor.**
- What it does: 75 x86 and GPU platforms, TensorFlow 2.5, CNNs (Cifar10-R18, DeepWeeds-R50v2, a small net). The same model and input give up to 26 different softmax vectors. Causes are SIMD width and core count on CPUs, and runtime convolution-algorithm autotuning on GPUs (39/99 GPU outputs nondeterministic). Metrics are equivalence classes (EQCs) and "remaining precision" (identical mantissa bits, 0 to 23).
- Diff: one-pass CNN. Server hardware (GCP, AWS, on-premises). Measures output deviation. Compute is fixed per input. It does not detect incorrect outputs. Precision is varied only as a mitigation (fp16 gives 3 EQCs, fp32 gives 9, fp64 gives 4). Training-free.
- Comparison with our claim: its deviations are ulp-scale. It says "label flips remain rare, the existence of deviations is not apparent in typical performance metrics". This is the noise floor under which our faults (0%, constant) are orders of magnitude larger. It gives layer-wise propagation: "deviations propagate between layers and may produce forks into different EQCs in later layers", but ReLU and pooling "can have a diminishing effect", and "past deviations may be cancelled out". So propagation in a one-pass net can shrink, which weakens any naive compounding story. It notes fixed-point conversion "reportedly eliminates all deviations", which fits our TFLite Micro constant-output bug being a bug rather than benign noise.
- Where we could fall short: our 0.67-0.73 AUROC across runtimes is this paper's cross-platform deviation floor. Without a calibrated tolerance, benign runtime differences look like faults.
- Take: cite as the benign-deviation baseline. Report "remaining precision" or an equivalent for our host-vs-device state gap, and place the broken builds many orders above it.

### 2. Louloudakis, Gibson, Cano, Rajan, "Fault Localization for Buggy Deep Learning Framework Conversions in Image Recognition" (2306.06157)
- Venue: ASE 2023 NIER track (new ideas), verified on the ASE 2023 program page; arXiv v5. Read: full (4 pages). Edinburgh and Glasgow; Rajan has prior refereed SE-testing work. Code via DeltaNN and FetaFix repos. **Grade: support** (NIER track, not main).
- What it does: converts MobileNetV2, ResNet101 and InceptionV3 among PyTorch, Keras, TF and TFLite. 11/36 conversions crash. 15 succeed but disagree with the source model, up to 100% (ResNet101 TFLite to Keras) and 49% (MobileNetV2 Keras to TFLite). Case study: TF to TFLite on InceptionV3 disagrees on 4% (240/5500) of images; per-layer activation differences localize the fault to TFLiteConverter weight-precision errors (up to 0.01).
- Diff: one-pass. Host and TVM, no device. Measures label agreement and per-layer activation differences. Detection needs a reference (the source model) and uses label discrepancy to pick the images to inspect. Training-free.
- Comparison: the closest prior statement of "convert, it runs, it silently disagrees". Layer-activation comparison against a source model is close to our early-state-vs-host check. Our check is label-free, build-level, and gives a detection AUROC. Theirs starts from label disagreement and localizes. Where we could fall short: its layer analysis also showed divergence from layer 2, so early-state comparison is not new in one-pass nets.
- Take: cite as precedent for "silent conversion faults" and for activation-level localization; position our check as detection, theirs as localization.

### 3. DeltaNN (2306.06208), Louloudakis et al., ICSME 2023 (pp. 414-424)
- Venue: ICSME 2023, Bogotá, from the Edinburgh research portal and authors' PDF; not checked against the IEEE page. Read: introduction, method and RQ1-2 results in full; timing results (RQ3-4) skimmed. Code: github.com/luludak/DeltaNN. Same authors as 2. **Grade: support** (ICSME is software-engineering, not an ML A*; Steven should decide whether to promote).
- What it does: a differential-testing framework over DL framework, TVM optimization level, and four GPUs (including AGX Xavier and Mali on a HiKey board), with MobileNetV2, ResNet101V2 and InceptionV3 on ImageNet. It finds up to 100% label difference across framework conversions and up to 81% inference-time degradation from compiler optimization. Compiler levels, including unsafe fast-math, gave no label differences.
- Quote of note: "the impact of these weight errors are cumulative, since weights are generally used repeatedly in multiply-accumulate operations." This is an untested assertion about accumulation within a pass.
- Diff: one-pass, GPUs (Xavier, Mali) rather than NPUs. Measures labels and time. It does not detect without labels.
- Take: a baseline pipeline (TVM plus differential labels). Shows compiler optimization was benign while the converter was the fault. This supports our "vendor toolchain, not float noise" framing.

### 4. Jajal et al., "Interoperability in Deep Learning: A User Survey and Failure Analysis of ONNX Model Converters" (2303.17708; v1 title "Analysis of Failures and Risks in Deep Learning Model Converters")
- Venue: ISSTA 2024, taken from the PDF's ACM reference block; not checked on the program page. Read: partial (survey, failure taxonomy, hypotheses, differential-test section; not every table). Purdue, Loyola; Davis has a long SE-for-DL record; artifact released. **Grade: anchor** (ISSTA is an A venue) once the venue is checked.
- What it does: survey of N=92 engineers; 200 closed converter issues. 74% of failures are in node conversion. Symptoms: crash 56%, wrong model 33%. It then differential-tests 3,522 models from HuggingFace against ONNX Runtime and finds converter failures in about 2% of them.
- Comparison: gives the base rate of silent wrongness for converters (one-third of failures). It states that tolerance for "acceptable error" is "not well established". It covers converters, not vendor quantizers or NPUs. It cites Shen et al. and NNSmith as the compiler side.
- Take: cite for the base rate and for the missing-tolerance problem (which our label-free threshold addresses).

### 5. Cruz Romero and Maldonado Guerra, "INT8 Quantization Makes ARM Edge Inference Dispatch-Invariant" (2607.23227)
- Exists. Preprint cs.ET, 25 Jul 2026. Capicú Technologies, two authors, no refereed record found, no code or artifact link in the text. Read: full. **Grade: caution** (no code, no record). Numbers are internally consistent and it is carefully hedged, so cite sparingly.
- What it does: Raspberry Pi 3B/4B/5/Zero 2W (A53, A72, A76), ONNX Runtime and TFLite+XNNPACK. FP32 on A76 differs between XNNPACK and CPU providers on 100% of images (mean 14.97 of 23 mantissa bits). INT8 QDQ gives byte-identical outputs and INT32 accumulators across SDOT and NEON paths for MobileNetV2 and ResNet50V2 on 500 images. The mechanism is exact integer accumulation; x86 diverges through PMADDUBSW saturation.
- Relevant points:
  - It reports a silent fallback: ONNX Runtime accepts XnnpackExecutionProvider without error but runs CPUExecutionProvider.
  - Its FP32 layer profile starts at Conv0 (2.56 bits) and attenuates downstream, which it calls "inconsistent with an error-accumulation story". For benign noise in a one-pass CNN, error shrinks.
- Comparison: for INT8 on ARM CPU, a correct build is bit-exact, so any nonzero deviation on an INT8 ARM build is a defect. This gives our TFLite Micro check a zero-tolerance oracle. Caveat: only 2 CNNs and one runtime family; not validated for REDUCE_SUM or loops.
- Take: use for the threshold (INT8 should be bit-exact), not as a headline.

### 6. Chen, "The Integer Alibi: Localizing Cross-Kernel Divergence in INT8-Quantized LLM Inference" (2608.13756)
- Exists. Preprint cs.LG, Aug 2026, single author at Krixvon (Taipei), cites two other 2026 papers by himself (e.g. 2608.11693). Code at github.com/luka-krixvon/integer-alibi; pre-registered; states a missing positive control and several limits. Read: full except the appendix. **Grade: caution** (single author, no refereed record, several 2026 preprints), with high internal rigor.
- What it does: on one RTX 4090, swapping vLLM's INT8 CUTLASS kernel for Triton changes every greedy continuation (0/8, 0/16, 0/64 sequences agree). INT32 accumulation is exact, so the divergence is in scale application and output rounding (at most one bf16 spacing). Power-of-two scales restore bitwise agreement. The logit margin predicts token flips at AUROC 0.94.
- Comparison: ulp-level differences, not catastrophic. It separates single-step flips from autoregressive cascade by teacher forcing, the nearest thing in this cluster to "tie size to iteration". Its limit 6 says none of its checks was tried against a deliberately faulty kernel. Ours is the opposite regime: gross faults, with a measured detection rate.
- Take: skip unless needed. Possibly cite for "exact integer accumulation implies any INT8 deviation sits after the accumulator".

### 7. Shin, "Is INT8 Portable? A Cross-Platform Measurement Study of Quantized Inference on Embedded and Automotive Accelerators" (2609.16085), found in search
- Preprint cs.AR, 14 Sep 2026; single author at KATECH (Korea Automotive Technology Institute); code and 32 reports released; limits stated (n=1 per hardware class, subsets). Read: introduction, related work, sections 6-10 and limits; sections 4-5, 7-8 skimmed. **Grade: support, leaning caution** (single author; no prior refereed record found).
- What it does: ONNX artifact and scales held fixed across seven hardware classes. INT8 predictions agree 1000/1000 when targets share an integer kernel and 958-965/1000 otherwise. Qualcomm Hexagon HTP silently ignores external QDQ scales (0.75 to 0.005), while the DEEPX compiler rejects the same graph loudly. Its words: "Qualcomm fails open (runs a silently broken model) ... DEEPX fails closed". It catalogs silent fallbacks (TensorRT EP listed but running on CPU; opset down-convert exits 0 with an invalid graph).
- Diff: one-pass CNNs and detectors. On-device measured. Detection of the fault is by accuracy against labels, found after the fact. No loops.
- Comparison: this is the nearest to our headline, both in phenomenon and in the Qualcomm silent collapse. Where we could fall short: it already says "fails open" and "a broken model can ship". It is concurrent (about 2.5 months before our deadline). Where we exceed it: 35 phones, 15 faulty configurations, four model families, a detector, a repair.
- Take: cite as concurrent, minimally. Its fails-open/fails-closed framing is useful vocabulary. Check our Qualcomm collapses are not the same mechanism (ignored external scales); if they are, narrow the claim.

## What this means for each claim

**Risk 1: our compounding explanation.** The ICLR submission (2609.39277, Kolawole et al.) finds fixed rounding error does not accumulate when the loop settles; it shifts the fixed point, "much as tilting a bowl". Cruz Romero (FP32 attenuation) and Schlögl (ReLU diminishes deviations) point the same way for benign noise. The ETH paper says a systematic bias does compound. A toolchain fault is a large systematic perturbation, so both views can hold, but the paper must say which. A safe wording: the fault is persistent and appears from the first loop; it is large enough to exceed the readout tolerance. Settle it with a planned (not run) analysis: plot host-vs-device state distance against loop index for each of the 15 faulty configs and classify growth, plateau or immediate offset. If most are flat after loop 1, drop "compound" and say "persist".

**Risk 2: trivial baselines.** If broken builds produce constant or 0% outputs, then output variance across inputs (Chen 2021's practitioner heuristic) or final-output distance to the host may already give AUROC 1.0. The 2-loop check needs these arms: final output vs host, constant-output test, one-loop state, and prefix-of-pass state for a one-pass model. Its advantage must be cost (2 of T loops) or the cross-runtime case; the 0.67-0.73 across runtimes is the weak spot (Schlögl's benign deviations plausibly explain it; Cruz Romero gives a zero-tolerance reference for INT8 ARM).

**How these papers detect:** differential testing against a reference (all), remaining-precision or bit identity (Schlögl, Cruz Romero), label agreement plus layer activation diffs (Louloudakis), PyTorch as oracle with bisection (NNSmith), exact-integer control and pre-registration (Chen). I found no use of metamorphic relations. Our 2-loop check adds: no labels, a decision per build, a state that exists only because the model loops. It lacks: localization, tolerance calibrated per runtime, and positive/negative controls like Chen's.

## Strongest honest novelty claim
Narrow it to this: first study of silent vendor-toolchain faults for iterative (looped, DEQ) models on edge hardware, at the scale of 35 phones, a Jetson, a TFLite Micro simulation and TensorRT, where fault severity (0%, constant output) is far above the cross-platform noise literature (Schlögl, Cruz Romero, Chen). Second, a label-free build-level check from the model's own early loop state against a host reference, with measured AUROC and honest cross-runtime limits. Third, repair by running the last loops at higher precision. Do not claim first demonstration of silent toolchain breakage (Shin, DeltaNN, Jajal), first label-free comparison against a reference (Kotla, FetaFix, NNSmith), or that errors compound across loops without the loop-index analysis.

## Ranked lists

**Three papers closest to our claims:** (1) Shin 2609.16085 (Qualcomm silent collapse, fails open); (2) Louloudakis 2306.06157 with DeltaNN 2306.06208 (silent conversion faults and activation-level localization); (3) Jajal 2303.17708 (wrong-model base rate).

**Three that most strengthen motivation:** (1) Schlögl NeurIPS 2023 (accuracy hides deviations; the noise floor to beat); (2) Jajal (33% wrong-model failures, tolerance undefined); (3) Wen 2511.11601 (heterogeneous accelerators silently disagree up to 14%).

**Claims our cluster leaves unclaimed:** silent faults in looped or DEQ models; the 35-phone and 15-configuration scale; a label-free build-level detector with AUROC from an early state; positional NPU-only fault; the TFLite Micro REDUCE_SUM constant-output bug (no study found); repair by high-precision final loops; TensorRT explicit-QDQ 0% across four models.

**Claims our cluster undercuts:** "silent toolchain fault" as a novel phenomenon (Shin, DeltaNN, Jajal); "label-free comparison to a reference" as a novel idea (Kotla, NNSmith, FetaFix); "errors compound across loops" (Cruz Romero, Schlögl, and our own ICLR paper); the early-state check as new for one-pass nets (Louloudakis layer analysis).
