## 1. FastTTS (arXiv 2509.00195v2) [reported as "FlashTTS" in your brief; the title is FastTTS]
1. Chen, Mo, Lu, Liang, Ma (Microsoft Research), Luk, Fan; Imperial College London. ASPLOS '26 (Pittsburgh, March 2026), DOI 10.1145/3779212.3790161. Venue verified only from the ACM reference line printed in the PDF, not from the program page. Code: artifact section present. Fan and Luk have refereed FPGA/LLM-acceleration work (from my knowledge, not re-verified). Slop flags: none. Proposed grade: ANCHOR (ASPLOS is an A/A* systems venue; confirm on the program page before citing as such).
2. A vLLM-based serving system for verifier-guided test-time scaling (beam search, best-of-N) on one consumer GPU (RTX 4090 24 GB; also 3070 Ti 8 GB and 4070 Ti 12 GB). Three techniques: speculative beam extension, asymmetric generator/verifier memory allocation, prefix-aware scheduling. Headline: 2.2x average goodput, 38-68% lower latency than vLLM; 7B+1.5B at n=512 up to 5.4x; 1.4-1.6x on the 8-12 GB cards.
3. Diff: autoregressive LLM + PRM verifier; real consumer GPUs (no phone, no NPU); measures goodput and latency, no energy; compute per input is a fixed search width n chosen by the operator, not decided per input; no error detection beyond the verifier's scoring; no precision change over time; training-free.
4. Comparison. It holds: "Instead of relying on scaling model parameters during training, TTS allocates additional compute during inference to improve generation and reasoning quality" and "strict memory constraints on edge devices limit deployment to smaller LLMs". Short against us: it is single-pass-per-token LLM serving, no loops, no energy, no per-input stopping, no fault study. Where we could fall short: its goodput accounting under a shared memory budget is more sophisticated than our per-step energy law, and a reviewer may ask why our cost law does not include KV/verifier memory. Settling analysis: state that our law covers loop count x per-step energy for a fixed-memory model, and add one line on what changes when state (not KV) is the memory object.
5. Take: the motivation sentence ("200 seconds of latency" for vLLM to match a cloud model on edge, Fig. 1b) and the memory-gap figure (Fig. 1a: edge-deployable 1.5B vs cloud models of hundreds of GB). Take their "a larger search budget (n)" curve as the thing our loop count is the iterative analogue of.

## 2. Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute with Mobile NPU on Smartphones (arXiv 2509.23324)
1. Hao, Wei, Wang, Huang, Jiang (MSR), Jiang (MSR), Cao (AIR Tsinghua), Ren (Tsinghua). EuroSys '26 (Edinburgh, April 2026), DOI 10.1145/3767295.3769382, from the ACM line in the PDF; not checked on the program page. Code released (github.com/haozixu/llama.cpp-npu). Ting Cao has prior refereed on-device LLM systems work (T-MAC, EuroSys 2025, from my knowledge). Slop flags: none. Grade: ANCHOR (EuroSys, A-level; verify on program).
2. Observes that decoding GEMV leaves the Hexagon NPU matrix unit idle, so parallel test-time scaling (best-of-N, beam search with PRM) is nearly free in latency. Contributes tile-aligned group quantization and LUT-based softmax/dequant. Up to 19.0x mixed-precision GEMM and 2.2x softmax speedup; Snapdragon 8 Gen 2/3/8 Elite (OnePlus Ace3, 12, Ace5 Pro). Claim: "smaller models using test-time scaling can match or exceed the accuracy of larger models, achieving a new performance-cost Pareto frontier."
3. Diff: autoregressive LLM (Qwen2.5 1.5B/3B, Llama3.2); measured on real phones (NPU); latency, normalised energy (power x relative decode latency; power under 5 W on OnePlus 12, performance mode), accuracy on MATH500/GSM8K; compute is the batch size N, set offline; no error detection; precision fixed W4 weight-only; training-free.
4. Comparison. This is the closest phone-NPU neighbour. It holds: "scaling compute resources at runtime requires even more computation" and "this is the first work to explore the feasibility and evaluate the trade-offs of test-time scaling methods for LLMs with NPUs on mobile devices" (their claim is about TTS on NPUs, not loops). Short against us: width (parallel samples), not depth; no per-input decision; no cost law beyond a normalised power x latency; no check of silent NPU faults beyond showing QNN per-channel quantization wrecks accuracy (Table 1: Wiki PPL 28.99 vs 19.42 AutoAWQ), which is a vendor-toolchain accuracy failure we could cite. Where we could fall short: they get "free" extra compute from an idle matrix unit; our loops are sequential and do not have that free lunch, so a reviewer will say depth costs more than width on a phone. Settling analysis: measure energy per correct answer for width-N vs L loops at equal accuracy on one phone, and say plainly where width wins.
5. Take: (a) the NPU-underutilisation argument as a counterpoint section; (b) Table 1 as an independent instance of toolchain accuracy failure; (c) their normalised-energy method as a baseline for our cost-law validation (we have error 0.5-1.1%, they have none reported); (d) "performance mode enabled" shows they never vary power mode.

## 3. EdgeReasoning (arXiv 2511.01866; IISWC 2025)
1. Kubwimana, Huang; NVIDIA. IISWC 2025 per the search listing (I did not open the IEEE page). Code: github.com/edge-inference/edgereasoning. Slop flags: none; two NVIDIA authors, Jetson AGX Orin, analytic models fitted with reported error (latency fit error 0.42-0.56% for decode/total on the three models). Grade: SUPPORT (refereed but a characterisation venue, not A*).
2. Characterises reasoning LLM deployment on Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB under latency budgets: reasoning vs non-reasoning models, size, token budgets, prompt-based length control, parallel test-time scaling. Fits latency and energy as functions of token counts (polynomial latency; log-fit decode power). Decode is 192-569x longer than prefill. Maps the accuracy-latency Pareto frontier.
3. Diff: autoregressive LLM (DSR1-Qwen 1.5B/14B, DSR1-Llama-8B, others); measured on device (AGX Orin); latency, power, energy, accuracy; compute decided by a latency budget (token cap, N); no error detection; all runs in MAXN (the paper lists 15 W/30 W/50 W/MAXN modes but "all experiments are conducted in MAXN"); training-free for the study.
4. Comparison. Closest on the deadline framing. It holds, near-verbatim: "(2) hardware-aware functions mapping latency budgets to maximum decodable tokens, and (3) continuous optimization across the latency-accuracy frontier", and "Discrete accuracy-latency tradeoffs fail to capture the continuous operational requirements of real-world systems". That is a compute budget per input tied to a deadline. Short against us: the budget is set per task class, not per input from a model signal; power mode is not varied; no loops; no faults; accuracy-latency, not energy per correct answer. Where we could fall short: they own the "latency budget to token budget function" idea; we must say our function maps loop count to energy and latency, per board and power mode, and that we vary the mode. Settling analysis: report our cost law as the same kind of function with a power-mode axis (they fixed MAXN), and show energy-per-correct-answer under 15/30/50 W.
5. Take: their Fig. 1 (robot examples: "Plan my weekly schedule" vs "Avoid that obstacle now!") is the best motivation for per-input budget; cite it for deadline-bound use. Their decode-dominance number frames why iteration counts matter more than prefill.

## 4. Understanding LLMs in Your Pockets (arXiv 2410.03613)
1. Xiao, Huang, Chen (Sun Yat-Sen University), Tian (Nanjing University). v5 Feb 2026, IEEE journal template; no published venue found. Grade: SUPPORT (measurement paper, no venue; credibility moderate).
2. Measurement study of LLM inference on COTS phones across Snapdragon, Dimensity, Apple; llama.cpp (CPU) and MLC-LLM (GPU). Covers throughput, memory, quantization accuracy, DVFS, thermal, battery. Reports 4-bit as the best accuracy/throughput balance and that thermal throttling raised battery temperature from 28 C to 47 C over five runs when run alongside YOLOv11n. Found "7B is the upper limit" under 8-16 GB RAM with at least 4 GB available.
3. Diff: single-pass LLM; measured on phones; latency, throughput, battery, thermal; fixed compute; no error detection; fixed precision.
4. Comparison. Holds only the motivation: on-device runs are constrained by DVFS, throttling and background load. Not about reasoning or compute budgets. Short against us on everything in our list.
5. Take: the 7B-upper-limit memory argument and the thermal throttling numbers as evidence that per-step energy is not constant, which supports our "per-step energy set by board, precision, power mode".

## 5. PowerInfer-2 (arXiv 2406.06282)
1. Xue, Song, Mi, Zheng, Xia, Chen; IPADS, SJTU. arXiv v3 Dec 2024; no conference venue found in two searches. The group's record is strong (PowerInfer, SOSP 2024). Grade: SUPPORT.
2. Neuron-cluster scheduling splits matrix ops across NPU (dense) and CPU (sparse), with a flash-I/O pipeline and a neuron cache so LLMs larger than DRAM run on a phone. 47B model at 11.68 tokens/s on a OnePlus; up to 27.8x over prior frameworks.
3. Diff: autoregressive LLM, sparse MoE; measured on device; tokens/s; fixed compute; no error detection.
4. Comparison. It is the opposite trade: spend flash I/O and keep a huge model on the phone. Quote: "only small language models (SLMs) can be deployed, significantly compromising model quality." Does not mention iteration or time-for-memory. No overlap with our claims.
5. Take: a contrast for related work: they enlarge the model beyond DRAM by streaming parameters; we shrink the model and iterate. Use as a one-clause "memory-side" baseline family.

## Answers to your questions
1. Systems framing and citable numbers.
 - FastTTS: "strict memory constraints on edge devices limit deployment to smaller LLMs, whose reasoning capabilities are much weaker"; vLLM needs about 200 s on a 4090 to match a cloud model's accuracy.
 - EuroSys 2509.23324: "scaling up the model size significantly increases memory consumption and bandwidth requirements"; decoding turns GEMM into GEMV and wastes the NPU matrix unit; phone power under 5 W.
 - EdgeReasoning: decode 192-569x prefill latency; "missed deadlines or no responses".
 - Qualcomm: memory bottleneck plus token-generation cost (power, latency, KV cache).
 - 2410.03613: 7B is the upper limit for 8-16 GB phones; battery temperature 28 to 47 C under load.
2. Is "trade time for memory" named as the on-device path? Only partially. MORES says it for recurrent latent reasoning ("trading parameter count for inference-time computation", "lower memory") but without device measurements. FastTTS and the EuroSys paper say it for sampling (width), not iteration. None measures loop count against memory on a device. A search snippet attributed to LoopCoder-v2 says looping "trades memory for time and energy"; I did not read it. So the exact framing (iteration instead of parameters, measured on phones/boards) is unclaimed in what I read.
3. Is the compute budget a runtime setting tied to battery, deadline or power mode? Deadline: yes in EdgeReasoning (latency budget to token budget, continuous). Power mode: listed but not varied (all MAXN). Battery: not as a control variable anywhere in the cluster; only measured in 2410.03613. Per-input from a model-internal signal: Qualcomm (a learned switcher), MORES (DRL on channel state). Nobody ties iteration count to power mode and battery together.
4. Framing moves to borrow:
 - EdgeReasoning's robot pair ("Plan my weekly schedule" vs "Avoid that obstacle now!") for per-input deadlines.
 - FastTTS's Fig. 1 memory-gap figure with the 8-24 GB limit.
 - The EuroSys "free compute is idle" hook, inverted: our loops use compute that costs energy, so energy per correct answer is the honest metric.
 - M4's "the stack was built for one pass" structure.
 - "Silent" failure story: the EuroSys Table 1 shows vendor INT4 per-channel quantization giving 28.99 vs 19.42 PPL without any error.
