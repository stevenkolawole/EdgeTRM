## 1. "Broken on Arrival: Non-Functional Quantized LLM Artifacts in Public Model Registries and How to Catch Them" (2609.05881v3)
- Aditi Patodiya, independent researcher (gmail address). cs.SE, v3 29 Oct 2026; "submitted to the IEEE", not accepted anywhere. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05881. Read in full (text, tables, threats, refs skimmed).
- Credibility: single author, no affiliation, no prior refereed record found; code and audit data released (github aditi-p31/quantcheck v3.4); AI use disclosed (Claude drafted text, scripts, ran experiments). v1 diagnosis (defective conversions) was retracted in v3 after a recipe-matched rebuild. Numbers are internally consistent and caveats are candid. Slop flags: single author, no affiliation, AI-written. Grade: **caution** (careful read done; method is sound, so citable as support-grade evidence only for its own narrow claim).
- What it does: runs all 305 eligible code-capable GGUF tags of 15 Ollama model lines plus 22 HF files on RTX 4090 (Metal replication on M2). A 15-task HumanEval+ screen flags suspects; 7 official tags (2.3%) solve <=6/164, all 2-3 bit tags of models <=4B. Five rebuilt from source match the registry file tensor for tensor (so not corrupted); an importance matrix lifts Qwen2.5-Coder-3B from 0 to 76-113/164. The same recipe leaves the 1.5B model working.
- Diff: model class = one-pass autoregressive LLM; hardware = desktop GPU and Apple GPU, no edge NPU; measured = HumanEval+ pass count, perplexity, output statistics; compute per input fixed; detection = yes, label-based screen (15 tasks with tests, ~30 s) and a reference-based perplexity check against the fp16 build (threshold 10-25x fp16, chosen after seeing the failures, false-positive rate unknown); precision fixed per file; no training.
- Silent? Yes: "The non-functional builds do not crash or refuse; they stream text at normal speed." Cause is the quantization recipe, not a vendor compiler bug.
- Label-free check and when it decides: perplexity vs fp16 needs a reference and full-file scoring on WikiText (20 chunks of 512 tokens). It decides after complete forward passes over text, not early in the computation. Output statistics (truncation, repetition) caught 4 of 7 suspects plus 6 false flags. No intermediate-state check.
- Our claim (c): different. It is about registry files of one-pass LLMs, a recipe/calibration failure rather than a compiler or runtime fault. Deciding quote: "None of these studies compares plain and calibrated builds of the same weights at the same level" (its own gap statement) and "Perplexity is not a functional verdict, though." It never touches loops, per-iteration state, or devices. What we take: (i) "the rule is a threshold from healthy builds" parallels our passing-build threshold; their post-hoc threshold is the weakness ours avoids; (ii) the cheap-output-statistics baseline (RQ3) is a baseline family for our detector comparison; (iii) a failing build yields fluent text with a normal speed, a good silent-failure illustration; (iv) Table I (execution studies) lists related work to mine.
- Where we could fall short: its "exact reproducibility does not imply function" overlaps our "build matches spec but is wrong" only loosely.

## 2. Shin, "Is INT8 Portable? A Cross-Platform Measurement Study of Quantized Inference on Embedded and Automotive Accelerators" (2609.16085v1)
- Yuyeong Shin, KATECH (Korea Automotive Technology Institute), cs.AR, 14 Sep 2026, preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16085. Read in full now (main text through section 10 and conclusion start; appendix and refs not read line by line; prior report only partial, this supersedes it).
- Credibility: single author, institutional affiliation, scripts and 32 reports released, limits stated candidly (n=1 per class, single-run p50, subsets). No refereed record in the area found. Grade: **support, leaning caution**.
- What it does: ONNX artifact and QDQ scales held fixed across seven hardware classes (Pi 5, Jetson AGX Orin CPU/iGPU/NVDLA, i.MX8M Nano, i9, RTX 3080, Qualcomm HTP on QCS8550/SA8775P via AI Hub, DEEPX DX-M1). Findings: INT8 speedup sign set by the dot-product ISA (0.6x to 2.1x); INT8 top-1 agreement 1000/1000 when two targets share an integer kernel, 958-965/1000 otherwise, with FP32 bit-identical; Qualcomm HTP ignores external QDQ scales (ResNet-50 top-1 0.75 to 0.005) while compiling, profiling, running without error; DEEPX rejects the graph; DETR INT8 mAP -42.9%; output size, not compute, sets NPU latency.
- Diff: model class = one-pass CNN/detectors (ResNet, YOLO, DETR); hardware = measured on device; measured = top-1/mAP, prediction agreement, latency; compute per input fixed; detection = none proposed, faults found by ground-truth accuracy and cross-target disagreement counts; precision fixed; no training.
- Silent? Yes for Qualcomm: "Qualcomm fails open (runs a silently broken model, dangerous, because a broken model can ship) and DEEPX fails closed". Also catalogs silent fallbacks (TensorRT EP listed but running on CPU, zero-copy output aliasing collapsing top-1 to 0.0014 "with no error", opset down-convert exits 0 with invalid graph).
- Label-free check: none. The only cross-target agreement measure needs the reference target's predictions for every input and runs on the full output after one pass. It proposes no detector and no repair; the power-of-two mitigation it tests fails.
- Our claim (c), corrected after reading Shin in full (Nov 24): Shin reports one silent collapse, an externally quantized ResNet-50 on Qualcomm's HTP whose external scales are discarded, fixed by Qualcomm's own quantizer. Our phone faults come from Qualcomm's own quantize jobs, the path Shin calls correct, and differ by device for the same graph, so they are a different fault. Our claim is also **broader** in scale and kind (35 phones, 15 faulty configs, six models, loops, TensorRT INT8 and QDQ, Jetson, TFLite Micro) and **different** in kind for the detector and repair, which it lacks. Deciding quote: "a bring-your-own QDQ graph fails silently on one NPU (external scales ignored, accuracy 0.75 -> 0.005 while it compiles, profiles and runs without error)". Do not claim first silent Qualcomm collapse. Its mechanism (external scales discarded) must be checked against our 12/80 phone failures; its own control (FP16 on HTP faithful, 0.745, vendor-native path 0.735) is the analogue of our host-vs-device test. Its "INT8 Jetson DLA is INT8-only, mandatory" supports our AGX forced-INT8 case. Its "SQNR does not predict accuracy (Spearman -0.030 over 21 layers)" is useful against a quantized-simulation reference (our 0.756 AUROC).
- Where we could fall short: it shows FP32 bit-identity across devices as a control; we should state our host fp32 reference is the same kind of control. Its 3-4% INT8 prediction flips across kernels are a noise floor our detector must sit above on passing builds (ours does by threshold from passing builds; show the passing-build spread).
- Experiment that settles whether our phone faults are the same mechanism as its: run our own QDQ-vs-AI-Hub-native-quantize path on one failing config and see whether native repair fixes it.

## 3. "Depth through Recurrence: Towards Ultra-Efficient On-Device ASR", Feng, Xu, Ramakrishnan, Lin, Zhuo, Yuan, Su, Zhang (Qualcomm AI Research)
- Venue verified: NeurIPS 2026 poster, Thu 10 Dec 2026, Hall 1-4 #S4501, page https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/149074 (track not stated). Slides: https://neurips.cc/media/neurips-2026/Slides/149074.pdf. **Read status: abstract and 8-slide deck only. No arXiv or full paper found** (search for an arXiv version returned none). Per the brief, not a read; mark cite=no until the paper appears. Corresponding email chenf@qti.qualcomm.com. Poster page lists "Jay Zhuo", the slides "Shaojie Zhuo": name inconsistency on the official pages.
- Credibility: Qualcomm AI Research, refereed NeurIPS 2026 venue (poster track unverified), no code seen. Grade: provisional **support** (anchor only after a full read and the track check).
- What it does: block-recurrent ASR encoder, K weight-shared blocks (K=2,4,8) applied n_k times, layers grouped by CKA similarity of the teacher plus DP, initialized by averaging teacher weights, distilled; MoE-LoRA personalization. Edge-STT K=2: 14.2M params, WER 13.40 vs teacher 11.05 (97.4% of teacher accuracy, 7.7x smaller encoder), K=4 10.72 (matches), K=8 8.88; random grouping at K=2 can collapse (WER ~0.9) while CKA-guided stays ~0.08.
- Energy/hardware: slides claim 1.46x lower DRAM energy, 1.28x lower GPU energy per inference and 1.21x lower latency at comparable FLOPs from weight reuse. The slides name a GPU, not a phone or NPU, so these are not on-device measurements as far as the slides show; no per-iteration cost, no fault study.
- Diff: model class = looped/weight-shared (fixed unrolled schedule, not input-adaptive; recurrence is a design dial, not stopping); hardware = GPU energy and DRAM figures (platform unverified); measures WER, params, memory/GPU energy; compute fixed per input; no detection of incorrect outputs; precision not varied; trained (distillation).
- Our claim (c): not covered (no faults, no toolchains). On our cost law: "Memory access costs far more energy than compute" (slide 8) and weight-reuse energy/latency gains at equal FLOPs support our finding that FLOP estimates miss energy by 2.2-8x. Deciding quote (slide 5): "At K=2, random grouping can collapse (WER up to ~0.9)" is a training-time fragility, not a toolchain fault. Take: a Qualcomm-authored looped model on device as motivation, and the energy-not-FLOPs framing as support for the cost law. Do not cite numbers beyond the slides until the paper is read.
