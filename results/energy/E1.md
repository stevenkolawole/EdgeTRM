# E1 — Energy on datacenter GPUs, the framework tax, and TensorRT as a toolchain correctness test

10567770, 10567771, 10568085, 10568086). Moved from p4d after Steven's 13:05 pause, on the coordinator's

is datacenter-GPU energy, not edge energy.** Platform of record: **Babel L40S** (one exclusive card per job,
clocks not pinnable, reported). An early partial Sudoku-MLP run on a p4d A100-SXM4-40GB is kept apart in
`EdgeTRM/results/energy/p4d_partial/` and is never put in a table with L40S numbers.

## Goal
1. Energy per step and per solved instance for TRM Sudoku-MLP, TRM Sudoku-attn, HRM Sudoku and TRM Maze at
   FP32 / FP16 / INT8, with and without the M5 halting exit.
2. Whether latency and energy rank configurations differently (INT8 vs FP16; exit vs fixed depth; batch 1 vs
   batched), including the framework tax at batch 1 (Fernandez et al., EMNLP 2023): eager PyTorch vs a
   TensorRT engine of the same step graph (plus eager + CUDA graph, which removes dispatch but keeps kernels).
3. TensorRT FP16/INT8 as a toolchain correctness test on X3's step graph: iterated carry chain (16 steps),
   exact accuracy, cell agreement and z_H cosine per step against the PyTorch fp32 reference. Does TensorRT
   INT8 collapse on Sudoku-MLP where ONNX Runtime and reference QDQ did (0/100), or hold as the S24 QNN HTP
   did (61/100)?

## Hypotheses (stated before any result)
- **H1 framework tax.** At batch 1 the eager step is dispatch-bound (a TRM H=3 step is ~430 TensorRT layers,
  several hundred eager kernel launches), so TensorRT is several times faster per step; the card sits near
  its idle floor while eager dispatches, so total energy tracks wall time and TensorRT also wins on energy.
  Idle-subtracted (dynamic) energy should shrink the gap, because eager's extra time is mostly idle power.
- **H2 INT8 vs FP16.** At batch 1 the GEMMs are tiny (97x512x2048 for Sudoku), so INT8 buys no time; TensorRT
  left to choose will pick FP16 kernels. Forced INT8 GEMMs add quantize/reformat layers and are at best equal,
  likely slower. At batch 256, INT8 GEMMs may save time and energy by <20%. Best case for the paper: INT8
  saves energy but not time somewhere.
- **H3 exit vs fixed depth.** The M5 rules save 2x (TRM Sudoku), 1.5x (HRM), ~8x (Maze) steps; the per-step
  host sync the exit needs costs little next to a step, so energy and latency per solved fall together.
- **H4 batch 1 vs batched.** Batched is far cheaper per puzzle in both energy and time (throughput); ranking
  by per-answer latency reverses it (a trivial disagreement, reported only for completeness).
- **H5 TensorRT INT8 correctness.** TensorRT's implicit INT8 keeps normalization reductions out of INT8 and
  (per its own warning) forces Reduce/Pow of layer-norm patterns to FP32, i.e. it keeps the RMSNorm
  variance path at high precision, which X3 found is what decides the w8a8 collapse. Prediction: TensorRT
  INT8 holds on Sudoku-MLP (within a few puzzles of fp32 on puzzles 0-99), unlike ORT / reference QDQ at
  literal 8-bit activations, even with INT8 preferred on every layer.
- Worst case: energy tracks latency everywhere.

## Design
- Code: `EdgeTRM/e1_energy.py` (stages prep, build, record, energy). p4d: code and engines in `~/e1/`,
  TensorRT 10.13.3.9 in `~/e1/pylibs/trt` (pip wheels, --target; the Windows builder resource deleted),
  nvidia-ml-py in `~/e1/pylibs/nvml`; torch 2.6.0+cu124, driver 595.71.05.
- Models at default depth and 16 supervision steps (as M5): TRM H=3 (Sudoku L=6, Maze L=4), HRM H=2 L=2.
  Correctness additionally at H=1 (X3's device configuration). Evaluation rows: Sudoku test 0-999, Maze
  test 0-899 (Maze rows 900-931 calibrate); calibration carries from fp32 rollouts on Sudoku test rows
  1000-1127, two random steps per puzzle (256 carries; Maze 64).
- Exit rules (M5 R1/R3, fp32-chosen): Sudoku-MLP two consecutive halts; Sudoku-attn native q_halt > 0; Maze
  min 2 steps + native; HRM its own q_halt > q_continue. Cap 16 steps.
- Graphs: X3's `stepc` export (carry in/out, logits, q_halt). TensorRT engines are built from a dynamic-batch
  re-export made with the same wrapper and checked against X3's file on real carries (`prep.json`: max abs
  difference 0 for Sudoku-MLP, <= 6e-5 elsewhere). Why not X3's static file directly: entropy calibration
  costs ~25 s per get_batch call on these graphs regardless of batch, so batch-1 calibration of 256 carries
  would take ~2 h per graph, and the calibration cache of the re-export does not transfer (516 tensor names
  differ). Provenance check: FP16 and INT8-GEMM engines built from X3's own H=1 file, calibrated directly on
  32 carries, are compared with the re-export engines.
- Engine precisions: fp32 (TF32 off), fp16, int8 (TensorRT's own per-layer choice, FP16 fallback), int8f32
  (INT8 + FP32 fallback), int8mm (INT8 required on every MatMul), int8all (INT8 preferred on every layer).
  Each engine's per-layer I/O precision is read from the engine inspector (`engines.json`).
- Energy protocol (ENERGY_PROTOCOL.md): NVML `nvmlDeviceGetTotalEnergyConsumption` on the job's exclusive
  Babel L40S (NVML handle matched to the CUDA device by UUID), 5 idle windows x 30 s with the process alive at the start and 2 at the end,
  10 s warm-up, then 5 windows >= 60 s per configuration of the looped workload; median of the 5. Before and
  after every window the card's compute-process list is checked (a window with any other process is marked
  dirty and excluded). Clocks are not pinned (default DVFS); SM/memory clock, power, temperature and
  utilisation are sampled at 10 Hz and reported per window. Accuracy and mean steps come from the outputs
  of the same run (first pass over all n puzzles; puzzles the timed windows did not reach are run untimed
  afterwards). Energy per solved = median energy per step x mean steps per puzzle / accuracy (energy per
  step is subset-independent; a single 60 s window covers only ~190 puzzles in the slowest configuration).
- Configurations per model (energy): batch 1 fixed 16 steps: eager fp32, eager fp16, CUDA-graph fp16, TRT
  fp32, fp16, int8mm; batch 1 with the exit: eager fp32, eager fp16, CUDA-graph fp16, TRT fp16, int8mm;
  batched (Sudoku 256, Maze 32), fixed 16 steps: eager fp32, eager fp16, TRT fp16, int8mm. TRT's own INT8
  choice (`int8`) is built and checked for correctness but not timed: on the p4d builds it placed no layer in
  INT8, i.e. it is the FP16 engine under another name.

### R1. TensorRT correctness (16-step carry chain vs the eager fp32 reference at the same H; `record.json`)

cell agreement 0.939, puzzle agreement 0.70, mean final z_H cosine 0.958; exact 71.2 vs 70.5), because batch size
changes reduction order and these trajectories amplify it. TensorRT fp32/fp16/int8 sit inside that floor. The H=1
fp32 chain agrees 1.0000 per cell with X3's ORT host fp32 chain at step 8 (Sudoku-MLP n=100, attn n=100, Maze n=50).

Same-puzzle comparison with the other two toolchains (H=1, puzzles 0-99 Sudoku / 0-49 Maze; device/ORT numbers
from REWRITE_NOTES 43-52, 46, 48): Sudoku-MLP int8mm 49 @8 steps and 67 @16 (X3's own file: 48 / 67; fp32 58 / 68)
vs S24 QNN w8a8 61 @8 and ORT/reference-QDQ w8a8 0 @16; Sudoku-attn int8mm 2 @8, 7 @16 (X3's file, 32-carry
calibration: 17 / 42) vs S24 50 and ORT 49; Maze int8mm 0/50 at every step vs S24 0/50 and host QDQ 42/50.
Readings: (a) **TensorRT INT8 holds on the MLP model and collapses on both attention models**, the mirror image of
ONNX Runtime (MLP 0, attention 49) and a third pattern next to the S24 HTP (MLP 61, attention 50, Maze 0). So no
single toolchain's INT8 verdict transfers: correctness is per (model, toolchain). (b) On Sudoku-MLP INT8 GEMMs drop
the z_H cosine to 0.71 (below the 0.958 floor) without costing a puzzle: the answer survives a carry that has
moved. (c) The INT8 GEMM set includes attention's QK^T and softmax·V matmuls, a likely locus of the attention
collapse (not isolated here). (d) Attention INT8 is calibration-sensitive (H=1: 8 vs 34.3/100 exact at 16 steps for
256 carries at batch 64 vs 32 carries at batch 1). (e) TensorRT's own INT8 mode chose FP16 for every layer on every
graph at batch 1 and batched: left to itself TensorRT does not run these models in INT8 at all. (f) INT8 preferred
on every layer does not build on any of the 4 graphs (no implementation for a Cast/Round node in the block): TRT
cannot express AIMET's all-8-bit graph.

Against the hypotheses:
- **H1 framework tax: confirmed, with a sharper form.** Eager FP16 vs TRT FP16 at batch 1 costs 8.8 / 9.9 / 14.0 /
  4.1x time but only 4.5 / 5.4 / 7.4 / 2.2x energy and 1.8 / 2.2 / 2.8 / 1.6x idle-subtracted energy: eager runs the
  card at 104-174 W (20-50% util), close to the 79-88 W floor, so most of the tax is idle-floor energy paid while
  the CPU dispatches. A CUDA graph of the same kernels removes most of it (dispatch alone: 6.2 / 4.9 / 7.3 / 2.4x
  time); TRT's kernels add 1.4-2.0x on top. On Maze, CUDA graph vs eager FP16 is 2.4x faster but uses 2% MORE
  dynamic energy (dyn ratio 0.976): the one place idle-subtracted energy ranks a runtime change differently.
- **H2 INT8 vs FP16: refuted in the "may save at batch" part; worst case holds.** INT8 GEMMs are slower AND costlier
  everywhere: Sudoku-MLP (the only model where they keep accuracy) 2.5x time / 2.0x energy at batch 1, 1.13x / 1.15x
  at batch 256. INT8 engines draw less power (180 vs 222 W, MLP b1) but run longer, so power ranks INT8 first and
  energy per solved ranks it last. No INT8-saves-energy-not-time case exists on this GPU.
- **H3 exit: confirmed, energy and latency fall together.** Exit/fixed per solved at equal accuracy (<= 0.6 pt):
  MLP 0.44-0.45x energy (0.43-0.47x time), attn 0.48-0.50, HRM 0.64-0.68 (0.66-0.72 time), Maze 0.13. The exit's
  per-step host sync costs 4% per TRT step (1.606 -> 1.670 ms MLP) but the card idles in it (222 -> 208 W), so the
  exit saves slightly more energy than time on TRT (MLP 0.440 vs 0.470; HRM 0.641 vs 0.723). Under INT8 GEMMs the
  degraded heads fire later (attn 13.7 steps, HRM 12.7, Maze never).
- **H4 batch: confirmed in direction; smaller in energy.** TRT FP16 batch 256 vs batch 1: 0.28x time but 0.41x
  energy per solved (MLP), 0.18 / 0.31 (attn); Maze batch 32 buys only 0.9x (seq 916 already fills the card), so
  batch-1 TRT with the exit beats batched fixed-depth Maze 7.0x in both. Sudoku-MLP batch 256 fixed vs batch 1 exit:
  0.60x time, 0.93x total energy, 1.18x dyn energy: batched wins on time, batch 1 + exit wins on work above idle.
- **Where latency and energy disagree (total card energy): eager FP16 vs eager FP32 at batch 1 on all three Sudoku
  models.** FP16 is 13-17% slower (a cast-heavy dispatch path) yet uses 8-15% less energy per solved (47-51% less
  above idle): MLP 1.166x time / 0.901x energy; attn 1.135 / 0.854; HRM 1.135 / 0.917. The same inversion appeared on
  the p4d A100 (separate platform, below). On Maze (compute-bound even at batch 1) FP16 wins both. Of the 61 compared pairs with finite
  values (`e1_tables.md`), total energy and latency order 58 the same way (the 3 exceptions are these), and
  idle-subtracted energy disagrees in 2 more (Maze CUDA graph; MLP batch 256 vs batch-1 exit); energy ratios are compressed
  toward 1 relative to time whenever the slower configuration leaves the card near its idle floor.

## What failed and what was done instead
- Batch-1 entropy calibration on X3's static graph: 25 s per sample (sudoku_mlp H=1, 8-sample probe), so
  256 carries would take ~2 h per graph; moved calibration to the dynamic re-export at batch 64.
- The re-export's calibration cache does not apply to X3's static file (516 tensors without scales, i.e.
  those engines silently had no INT8 layers); those engines were deleted and replaced as described above.
- TensorRT entropy calibration spent ~25 s per get_batch call regardless of batch size (TRT 10.13, these graphs).
- `int8all` (INT8 preferred on every layer) fails to build on Sudoku-MLP: TensorRT's Myelin backend cannot type a
  Round on an INT8 elementwise Mul inside the MLP block ("Could not find any implementation for node
  {ForeignNode[/inner/L_level/layers.0/Cast.../Transpose_1]}"); recorded as a build failure (a toolchain result).
- On p4d, TensorRT's enqueueV3 on the legacy default stream added synchronisations (its own warning); every runner
  now uses a fresh non-default stream, and the p4d TRT windows taken before the fix were discarded.
- Engine builds were far faster on Babel (level-1 optimisation for correctness-only engines; calibration seed
  45-379 s) than on the shared p4d card (218-1348 s).
- TensorRT left to choose (`int8`) put **no layer** in INT8 at batch 1 or batch 256 on Sudoku-MLP (every
  layer FP16; `engines.json`), so the int8mm / int8all variants were added to actually exercise INT8 kernels.

## Grid (MLSys, Sep 26; queued after a smoke job): hypotheses stated before any result
Grid per model on one exclusive L40S: {eager fp32, eager fp16, TRT FP16, TRT INT8 = int8proj, ORT CUDA EP fp16}
x batch {1, 8, 64, 256} x {fixed 16, halting exit} + head-gated polish at batch 1 (TRT INT8 under the exit rule, then
TRT FP16 polish until its head fires, <= 4 steps). Continuous batching (a finished slot is refilled at once); energy
per solved puzzle and latency (slot fill -> answer) with 95% t-intervals over 5 windows of >= 60 s; idle subtracted.
`EdgeTRM/e1_grid.py`, records `$HOME/e1/results/<task>/grid.json`.
Launches: smoke `20_E1_gridsmoke` (job 10570069, preempt; 3 s x 2 windows, 8 Sudoku-MLP configs incl. ORT CUDA,
continuous-batching exit and polish: all ran, accuracies 70-75%; numbers under `~/e1/smoke`, NOT results; fix: ORT
intra-op threads set explicitly after affinity errors). Grid: `~/autofill/babel_backlog/preempt/20_E1_grid_{sudoku_mlp,
sudoku_attn,hrm_sudoku,maze}.sbatch` (one model per GPU, --requeue, resumable per configuration). F2 rerun
`10_E1_qdq2` (job 10570068).
- **G1 (INT8 on the L40S).** int8proj is slower and costlier than TRT FP16 at every batch, as int8mm was, and more
  so at batch 1 (the extra quantize/reformat layers dominate a launch-bound step); if it wins anywhere, it is at
  batch 256 on the MLP-heavy Sudoku-MLP graph, by < 15%.
- **G2 (exit under batching).** At batch 1 the exit saves energy and time together (as measured: 0.44-0.13x). Under
  continuous batching the saving survives but shrinks at batch 256 (one host sync per step and the rule's per-slot
  bookkeeping now sit on a much shorter step), and energy saving exceeds time saving wherever the sync leaves the card
  idle.
- **G3 (batch).** Energy per solved falls monotonically with batch for every toolchain on Sudoku; latency per puzzle
  rises. Maze saturates the card by batch 8 (seq 916), so batch 64/256 buy little energy and cost latency.
- **G4 (framework tax vs batch).** The eager/TRT gap is largest at batch 1 (dispatch-bound) and shrinks towards the
  kernel-quality ratio (1.4-2x) by batch 256. ORT CUDA fp16 sits between eager and TRT at batch 1 (it removes Python
  but not per-op launches) and near TRT at batch 256.
- **G5 (polish).** Head-gated FP16 polish after INT8 recovers nothing on these graphs (int8proj is already within
  ~1 point) and costs more than FP16 alone, because INT8 steps are the slow ones here: the M5 "cheap map" premise
  inverts on a datacenter GPU.
- **G6 (rankings).** Energy and latency per solved agree except (a) eager fp16 vs fp32 at batch 1 (seen before), and
  (b) batched vs batch-1 with exit, where total energy and idle-subtracted energy can order them differently.
